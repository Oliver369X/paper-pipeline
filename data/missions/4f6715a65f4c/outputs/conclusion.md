This replication study confirms that the **low‑curvature directions in the K‑FAC‑style decomposition of MLP weights exhibit a spectrum of behaviors**, where the flattest components are strongly associated with narrow memorization and brittle arithmetic or fact‑retrieval, while higher‑curvature directions support more robust, general‑purpose reasoning. Across several small‑scale LMs, we reproduce the core finding that **editing out low‑curvature modes suppresses targeted and untargeted recitation** with relatively small costs on perplexity and generalization, and we verify that methods like Bal‑ancedSubnet achieve similar unlearning but with different trade‑offs. This implies that the core methodology is **accessible without massive GPU resources**, enabling broader replication and mechanistic exploration in the community, while leaving the scaling to large‑scale models, multimodal‑ViTs, and continual‑learning settings as a natural next frontier.
