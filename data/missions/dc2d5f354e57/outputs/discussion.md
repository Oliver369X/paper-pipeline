## Relation to Prior Work
BalancedSubnet extends Subnet unlearning by incorporating a balanced objective that penalizes masks affecting non-memorized tasks, addressing limitations in neuron- vs. weight-based methods from works like Chang et al. (2024) and Maini et al. (2023). Unlike regularizers (e.g., spectral norm, loss truncation) or fine-tuning, which trade off effectiveness or efficiency, BalancedSubnet achieves superior memorization reduction (near -100%) with minimal perplexity/accuracy loss, as shown in TinyMem and Pythia models. Recent surveys highlight its alignment with emerging LLM unlearning paradigms, though activation steering and gradient ascent methods offer complementary approaches for specific privacy threats.[2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.06040)[3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.02159)[4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.01854)[1](https://www.stat.berkeley.edu/~mmahoney/pubs/12369_Mitigating_Memorization_.pdf)

## Broader Impacts for Safety and Alignment
Reducing memorization via BalancedSubnet enhances AI safety by curbing regurgitation of sensitive data, backdoors, or copyrighted content, directly mitigating privacy risks under regulations like GDPR. For alignment, it supports scalable unlearning of misaligned artifacts without full retraining, promoting robust deployment in agentic systems where persistent memory could amplify behavioral drift or deception. This enables safer fine-tuning pipelines, especially for open-source models in resource-constrained settings like Bolivia's AI research labs.[5](https://bohrium.dp.tech/paper/arxiv/2402.10058)[6](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/when-ai-remembers-safety-agent-implications-models-adrian-wilson-lbzjc)[7](https://www.emergentmind.com/topics/safety-alignment-capabilities)[1](https://www.stat.berkeley.edu/~mmahoney/pubs/12369_Mitigating_Memorization_.pdf)
