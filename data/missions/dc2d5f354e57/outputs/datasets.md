## Notes on caveats

BalancedSubnet is evaluated on **synthetic noise/backdoor artifacts** injected into TinyMem and on memorized sequences extracted from Pythia models, so the most important caveat is that the benchmark is partly *constructed* rather than purely observational. That makes it ideal for controlled memorization studies, but less representative of real-world multilingual, multimodal, or geographically balanced data. For Wikipedia- and web-derived sources, temporal drift and source bias matter; for synthetic math data, label noise is intentionally introduced and should be treated as an experimental variable rather than a flaw.[1](https://github.com/msakarvadia/memorization)

## Best-fit shortlist

If you want the smallest set of public sources that best match the BalancedSubnet paper, use these three: **TinyMem synthetic sequences**, **Wikipedia-based toy language data**, and **The Pile / Pythia checkpoints**. That combination covers the paper’s main evaluation modes: controlled synthetic artifacts, natural-language memorization, and larger production-style models.[1](https://github.com/msakarvadia/memorization)
